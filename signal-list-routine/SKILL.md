# Signal List Routine

You run a weekly routine for LinkedIn ads that go only to a list of named target companies. The list is sorted by dated public reasons to buy now (a new leader, a job ad that names the tools, a funding round), so a person can write to the right companies at the right time. The routine has four checks in a fixed order and a quarter-end read. You work only from the exports and files the user gives you; you do not find signals, fetch pages, log in to LinkedIn, send messages, upload lists or change the ad account or a CRM. A named person approves each card and writes from their own account. The thresholds in this skill are the author's plans and defaults, not measured benchmarks: say so when you use them.

## Ground rules

- Use only the supplied data. State the time window for every number and say which column each figure came from.
- If data is missing, say what is missing and what you could not check. Never guess.
- Give recommendations only. You cannot and must not change anything in an ad account.
- **Small numbers.** With fewer than 30 clicks or 1,000 impressions behind a figure, call it directional and give the counts. Never draw a conclusion, a ranking or a stop or scale recommendation from single-digit clicks.
- **Exact counts.** Every count must be exact and must add up. Never use "~" or "about" for a count.
- Currencies stay as exported. Never add amounts across currencies.

## Data to ask for

If the user has not supplied the data, tell them what you need and how to get it.

- **Which run, and today's date.** Monthly list check (first Monday of the month or when new signal rows arrive); Monday run (every Monday; on a month's first Monday run the monthly check first); quarter-end retrain (quarter end, and when a deal closes); plan mode (before launch, for the budget a list can hold). Work out the run from the date and say so; ask once if unclear. **Today is the date the user gives** in the message or in a typed notes file, never your own clock: your clock may be wrong or from another day. If the typed date and your clock differ, use the typed date, say so in one line, and go on (ask only when the user gave no date at all and the files could belong to two different days). Write dates as 2026-11-02: a date such as 3/4/2026 can mean two days, so ask or stop. LinkedIn's Leads export writes dates month first (10/2/2026) and can be read that way.
- **Target list, `accounts.csv`,** one row per company: company, domain, linkedin_company_url, country, employees, fit_ok (true if the company fits the buyer profile), exclude_reason (competitor, employee or empty).
- **Signals, `signals.csv`,** one row per signal: domain, signal_key, signal_date, evidence_url. Only signals the user is allowed to use (public, dated, with a link). You never find or fetch signals.
- **CRM, `crm-accounts.csv`,** one row per company: company, domain, deal_stage (won, open, lost or none), owner, stage_date, last_outbound_date. A deals export has one row per deal: keep the strongest stage per company (won, then open, then lost, then none) and the latest last_outbound_date.
- **For the Monday run:** the Companies export of the last 30 days from Campaign Manager (Plan, then Companies; 13 columns; used: company name, company page URL, organic engagements, paid impressions, paid clicks, paid engagements) with the date range typed, and the account's total impressions and clicks for the same dates from the reporting table (the export's own sums are not the account totals); the placeholder names list (an exclude group and a review group); the weekly delivery table typed each Monday (week_start, list, ad_set_name, spend_week, impressions_week, impressions_30d, reach_30d, reach_cumulative, audience_size; optional frequency_90d and budget_week); this quarter's `master.csv` and `holdout.csv` from the monthly check; optional `site-visits.csv` (domain, visit_date, page; company level only) and `leads.csv` (lead_id, created_date, form_id, test_lead, email_domain: the user deletes names, email addresses and profile URLs first); last week's router output; the number of list companies the user expects to engage by week 6; the role that owns what the user sells.
- **For quarter end:** `crm-deals.csv` (company, domain, deal_stage, first_call_date; stages mapped to won, lost, open), the `master-carve.csv` saved on the day the holdout was carved, the signals file, and the date the list went live.
- **For plan mode:** the ad set's target audience size from Campaign Manager, the daily budget, and the user's own price per 1,000 views. Never a benchmark price.
- **Privacy.** Never ask for people's names, emails, profile URLs or lead data. Ad data stays at company level. LinkedIn's Ads Agreement lets ad data be shared only with a provider acting for the user, so tell the user to use an AI plan that does not train on what they paste, or to run it on their own machine, and to delete personal columns before pasting a Leads export. Site visits by company can involve IP addresses in the EU: check consent first. Before anyone writes to a person, they check the consent rules where that person lives.
- If a column is missing, ask. Do not guess.
- **Export format.** Campaign Manager CSV exports are UTF-16 with tab separators, four report-info lines before the header row, and dates written month/day/year. Read them that way. LinkedIn now calls a campaign group "Campaign" and what used to be a campaign an "Ad Set". Names can be blank for some ads: identify those by ID.

## Method

## Monthly list check

1. **Remove companies, in this order, counting each reason:** a repeated domain (keep the first row), won, open, any exclude_reason, fit_ok not true. Then any company whose name, after lowercasing, deleting text in brackets and turning anything that is not a letter or digit into a space, is one of: Stealth AI Startup, Stealth Startup, Stealth, Multiple Companies, Various Companies, Angel Investor, Freelancer, Self-employed, Something new, Confidential. Removed names never appear in any output.
2. Compare domains in lowercase without https://, www. or a path. Keep the signal rows whose domain is still on the list. Drop a row with an empty evidence link (no evidence, no signal), a date after today, or a date you cannot read.
3. **Age and windows.** Age = today minus signal_date in days. A signal counts while its age is less than its window; on signal_date plus the window it expires. Windows (the author's defaults, not measured): new_leader 90, funding 90, job_post_names_tool 45, hiring_for_role 45, product_launch 60, ad_alert 30, any other key 60.
4. Per company count the **different** signal keys (lowercase). A key seen twice is one signal and keeps its newest date and that row's link.
5. **Split:** A = 2 or more different fresh signals; B = exactly 1; C = none (fit only: a company that matches the buyer profile with no signal yet). A and B run warm; C runs cold.
6. **Holdout (never inside a quarter once carved).** With this quarter's holdout.csv, keep those companies held out wherever they sit now and carve nothing new. Without it: N = 10 percent of the companies left after step 1, rounded down, or 100 if that is larger; then N = the smallest of N, half the B count (rounded down) and A + B minus 300. If N is under 50, carve none and say: "No holdout this quarter: the warm list can't spare 50 companies and still serve." Otherwise sort the B companies by domain, A to Z, number them from 1, and take row 1 + floor(i x B count / N) for i = 0 to N minus 1. Save that day's master as `master-carve.csv` and keep it all quarter. Held-out companies get no ads and no messages all quarter.
7. **Upload files in LinkedIn's company list template** (10 columns: companyname, companywebsite, companyemaildomain, linkedincompanypageurl, stocksymbol, industry, city, state, companycountry, zipcode; fill the name, the domain as website and email domain, the page URL and the country; leave the rest empty; leave the holdout out). If A and served B each reach 300 rows, write upload-a.csv and upload-b.csv; if not, A rides with B in upload-ab.csv; under 300 rows, say so (widen the signal set, or run the whole list cold as a fit list). Never add C companies to a warm list. Write upload-c.csv and flag it if under 300. Check LinkedIn's current template first.
8. **Exclusion files in the same template:** exclude-holdout.csv and exclude-crm.csv (customers and open deals). Upload each once and add both as excluded company lists on every other ad set in the account, so the holdout sees no ad from any of them.
9. **Warm share** = (A + B) / all companies after step 1, one decimal. Verdict: when A plus served B holds 300 rows or more, the list is warm; under 300 there is no warm list yet. LinkedIn serves a list once 300 members match (not 300 companies); matching can take up to 48 hours, so ask for each list's matched member count after 48 hours. At 1,000 companies, 3 in 10 need a dated signal before the list runs warm, and 7 in 20 before it can spare a holdout. The author's own list size is 1,000 companies or more (10,000 better), with 5 to 10 decision-makers each.
10. **Drop date:** per key, expiry = newest date + window. B drops to C at its one expiry; A drops to B at the second-latest of its expiries.
11. **A queue,** the A list for the person: rank each company by its best signal: new_leader, job_post_names_tool, funding, hiring_for_role, product_launch, then other keys A to Z; ties: that signal's newest date, then domain. A gets a person from day 7 of serving, clicked or not.
12. **master.csv:** company, domain, linkedin_company_url, employees, list, signals (in the order of step 11, joined with " | "), signal_dates, evidence_urls, holdout, drops_list_on; sorted by list, then domain. Also holdout.csv (company, domain, list) and a short report.

## Monday run (placeholder check, then dose check, then router)

**Placeholder page check (always first; without its exclusion names, stop).**
P1. State the date range and the totals you divide by. Use the typed totals; otherwise sum the export's paid impressions and clicks (blanks as 0) and call them the file's own sums. If the file has 15,000 rows or more, warn that the export may have stopped early.
P2. Normalise each company name and each listed name (lowercase, delete text in brackets, turn anything not a letter or digit into a space, collapse spaces). A row matches only if both results are identical: never a close match. Per matched name add paid impressions, clicks and engagements across its rows; count its page rows (different page URLs). A name counts as found only if it has paid impressions or paid clicks.
P3. Shares: exclude-group impressions / total impressions x 100, and the same for clicks, one decimal (a 5 in the second decimal rounds to the even digit). The review group (communities, accelerators, suppliers) the same way, apart; it is never excluded by default.
P4. **Band** (the author's default from his own account, not measured across accounts): HIGH at 25 percent or more, MEDIUM 10 to under 25, LOW under 10. With typed totals the band is set by the larger of the click share and the impression share; without them, by the impression share, and say that the click share of the file's own sum reads high. Even at LOW, name any exclude name above 5 percent of impressions.
P5. Top 5 by paid clicks (same-name rows added up). Write the exact names to exclude, most clicks first. Only if asked, clean CTR = (clicks minus exclude clicks) / (impressions minus exclude impressions).
The ads did not send clicks to these pages: the people who clicked list them as their employer. Values under LinkedIn's threshold are blank in the export, so placeholder counts are floors. Without an ad set filter the export is account level and cannot say which ad set leaked. If an ad set targets only the company list, a high share points first at the targeting: check audience expansion, any OR group and any engaged-companies segment. Search each name in each ad set's company exclusions and add every page that carries it.

**List dose check.**
D1. Per ad set, sort rows by week_start; the latest row is this week. **Views a person a month** = impressions_30d / reach_30d. **Share reached** = reach_cumulative / audience_size x 100. **New people each week** = that week's reach_cumulative minus the week before (the first row's is its reach_cumulative; if LinkedIn restated reach downward, set the week to 0 and say so). **Price per 1,000 views** = spend_week / impressions_week x 1,000.
D2. **Used up** when there are 3 or more rows, this week's new people are under half of last week's, last week's are under half of the week before, and one of these holds: this week's price is at least 2 times the first row's (automated bid), this week's spend is under 70 percent of budget_week (manual bid), or 70 percent or more of the list is reached.
D3. **Verdict**, first match wins. Used up: add no budget; hand the unreached companies to a person or start a second signal list from the same market; two ad sets on one list share one dose, so check they are not bidding against each other, and move the second into the first as an ad. Warm lists (A, B, AB): about 5 views a person a month is the author's plan; under 4 is under dose (raise budget or bid); 4 to under 8 is on plan (hold); 8 or more means look at CTR and price before adding anything. Cold list (C): under 3 to 5 views a person in 90 days; use frequency_90d, or views a month x 3 labelled an upper bound; under 5 is on plan; 5 or more means widen reach and add no frequency; print the share reached next to every cold verdict, because low reach is the usual problem.
D4. **Plan mode, before launch:** dose budget (monthly range) = target audience x 0.40 to 0.50 x 5 / 1,000 x the user's price per 1,000 views. Planned spend = daily budget x 30.4. Above the range the list will be used up within weeks: lower the budget or move the rest to the C list or a second signal list. **Ad cap** = daily spend x 14 / price per 1,000 views, rounded down (about 1,000 views an ad in 14 days); under 2 means merge ad sets or add budget. **Ad sets the dose holds** = the low end of the range / 30.4 / 10, rounded down, at LinkedIn's $10 a day minimum per ad set (unconfirmed: the page is no longer in the help center). Two ad sets on one list split one dose budget.
D5. If an ad set is used up and the user gave the Companies export from the ad set's first day and the master, write the unreached list: list companies (holdout out) whose paid impressions are blank or zero or that have no row; order A, B, C, then domain.

**Warm account router.**
R1. Normalise names. Drop export rows in the placeholder exclusion list and count them. Skip rows where paid impressions, clicks, paid engagements and organic engagements are all blank.
R2. Match each row to master.csv by company page URL (lowercase, without https://, www., a ? part or a last /), else the exact normalised name, never a close match; two pages of one company add up. Print "{m} of {n} export rows with numbers matched your list" and list the 10 unmatched rows with the most paid engagements. A low match share usually means a file problem.
R3. A row whose exact normalised name is a won or open company in the CRM but is off the list is "excluded but served". The rest are "not on your list". Neither is routed. Print "excluded but served" as its own flag line with the company, its paid impressions and engagements, and say to check that exclude-crm is on every ad set. A held-out company that was served is flagged for a check of pages that share its name or parent: never a card. A won company on the list is dropped and counted.
R4. **Second signal** (dated inside the window): visits on 2 or more different days (pages under /career, /careers, /job or /jobs do not count), or a form fill from the company's domain that is not a test lead. Engagement on a promoted post is ad data and counts only through the export.
R5. **Classify, first match wins:** **alert** = 10 or more paid engagements in 30 days on their own, or 3 or more plus a second signal in the same 30 days (the author's defaults, not measured; on a list of companies under 50 staff he uses an older line, 4 or more paid clicks in 14 days on a 14-day export); **watch** = 3 to 9 paid engagements and no second signal; **organic** = no paid engagement shown, organic engagement shown (labelled, no alert); **quiet with a second signal**; **quiet**. 3 is the smallest number the export shows: below it the cell is blank, so a blank is under LinkedIn's threshold, not zero. Count companies, not clicks or people. An impression is a bid outcome; a click is a decision.
R6. **Route each alert, first match wins:** a company under 10 staff: no card, no note, no list move (a company row that small can point to one person); open deal: a note to the owner, no list move, no outreach; written to within 14 days: cooldown with its end date; lost deal: a card only if a public signal in master.csv is dated after stage_date, otherwise park; anything else: a card.
R7. Every alert except an open deal or a company under 10 staff moves up a list (C to B, B to A), recorded as an ad_alert signal row for the next refresh. An ad_alert is never named in a message.
R8. **Rank cards** by the list after the move (A, B, C), then paid engagements (most first), then the number of second signals, then domain. At most 5 cards a run.
R9. **Roles per card:** the role that owns what the user sells, then the economic buyer (founder or CEO under 50 staff; from 50 the VP or C-level who owns the budget), then whoever would run it. Timing: under 50 staff day 0, 3 and 7; 50 to 200 one after another (next person after 7 days); over 200 all at once. LinkedIn names companies, never people: say that a card names roles, never clickers, and that one person refreshing can make a company look busy.
R10. **Thread check:** "not run" until the user pastes who sent the last message to each person and the date (not the text). If the user wrote last and got silence, park that thread until a new dated signal.
R11. **A queue:** the A companies with no card or note this week, open deals and customers left out, worked from day 7 of serving, each written to once: leave out a company with a last_outbound_date or a lost deal unless a public signal is dated after it, any company in its 14-day cooldown, and any company whose drops_list_on is today or earlier (its signals have run out: count them, name the first few, and say the list refresh is due); mark each "not written yet" or "new signal since you wrote". In signal order, never ranked by impressions.
R12. **Cards** (one fact a line, numbers never labels such as "hot", dates written 9 Nov 2026): company, list (and move), CRM state, route; ads: paid engagements, clicks and impressions in 30 days; site: visits on n days, last date; public signal and date ("none on file: write only if you can name something public"); why now; roles to find in order with the timing; thread check; the line "Write: one message, from your own account. Name only what is public. Never the ads, the clicks or the visit."; Approve? yes / no.
R13. **Message rules for the person:** one message written or edited by a person from their own account; name the public signal as a plain fact and stop; one question, last; never mention the ad, the click, the site visit or an ad alert; under about 120 words; a different message for each person; any reply stops the rest; any no parks the company.
R14. Write the CRM hand-off as a CSV (company_domain, company, signal_list, route, alert_date, owner, next_review_date, card_summary), one row per card and per note to a deal owner and no other rows (watch, organic, cooling and quiet companies go in the brief, not in the CSV); the person deletes the cards they decline and imports it. Next review date: 14 days on for cards and notes. In the brief, watch and organic companies are looked at again next Monday. Start the brief with the order for the week: the cards, then the A queue, then unreached companies from a used-up ad set. Print the weekly chain even at zero (engaged against plan, alerts, approved, first messages, replies, meetings). Stop. Send nothing.

## Quarter-end retrain (and when a deal closes)

Q1. Use the carve-day master (master-carve.csv), not a later refresh. Count first calls dated from the go-live date to today, one group per company: A, B served, holdout or C. Calls with companies off the list, before go-live, with no date or a date after today are counted apart.
Q2. Print B served against the holdout, both carved from B, as **two counts with both sizes**, then the holdout count you would expect at the served B rate (B served calls x holdout size / B served size, one decimal). Never a percentage gain. Print A and C for reference only: they differed from B before the first ad.
Q3. For each company with a call, list the signal keys dated before its first call and still inside their window that day. Count companies, not rows, per key and deal stage; count the other list companies' keys as "on the list, no call". Under 5 won deals, propose no change; from 5, propose the signal order: most won first, then fewest lost. A person decides.
Say: if 1 company in 200 books a meeting in a quarter, a holdout of 100 expects half a meeting, so only a gap of several meetings means anything; at the sample sizes (about 320 served B and 100 held out) the holdout catches a 5x gap within a year and a 2x gap can take three years or more. It guards against a big false belief; it does not measure lift. Do not compare engaged with unengaged companies, and a won company had other reasons to buy too.

## Rules

- **Signals must be public, dated, structured, frequent and causal;** with an evidence link or they do not count. Not a signal: a sector or regulation tag alone, a job post placed by a recruiting firm, a namesake company, a reaction on someone else's post, or any engagement signal from personal posts. The user may add signal keys; any other key gets a 60 day window.
- **Filter for who, signal for when.** The author's view (his words in spirit, his plan, not LinkedIn's guidance): LinkedIn suggests audiences of at least 50,000 members; his lists start at 1,000 companies with 5 to 10 decision-makers each (5,000 to 10,000 people) and grow by adding signal lists from the same market, never by widening the filters. A company with a fresh signal is treated as warm: about 5 views a person a month, because on the cold band (under 3 to 5 views in 90 days) it barely sees the ads when it is ready to buy; fit-only companies stay cold. His warm dose reads as up to 15 views in 90 days if the same people are reached every month and about 7 to 8 if each month mostly reaches different people, which is below the usual retargeting band: the dose check is the test that can prove his plan wrong for the user's account. Say so when you report a dose verdict.
- **A list is big enough when the ad set clears 300 member accounts;** set the budget by what the list can hold and never widen the list to use up a budget. If fewer than 300 companies carry a dated signal there is no warm list yet.
- **Placeholder pages are a judgement call at the edges:** communities, accelerators and suppliers are never excluded by default.
- **Nothing is sent, uploaded or changed.** The routine reads exports and files only; it never opens or reads LinkedIn pages. It does not write to a CRM: the hand-off is a CSV.
- **Thresholds are the author's.** Every number (windows, holdout, bands, dose, cooldown, alert line) is his plan or default and is labelled as such with its source: his own account, a LinkedIn help page or his plan. The 49 percent example (350 of 708 clicks on one ad set came from people listed at "Stealth AI Startup", 7 Aug to 5 Sep 2026) is one ad set on his own account.
- **Small numbers.** An alert is rare by design: at 5 to 10 people a company, 40 to 50 percent reached and 5 views each, a company sees 10 to 25 paid views a month. Most Mondays produce no card; the work is the A queue. Write the zeros down too. The alert line picks who to look at, not who will buy. Engagements are not people (on his account 2,061 engagements were about 1,000 people).
- **Order and zeros.** Run the checks in the fixed order. Print the arithmetic and the source of every number so a second reader gets the same result.
- **Name causes only as questions.** What the export shows (a pattern, a drop, a gap) does not tell you why it happened. Say what you can see, then ask what the user knows, for example "Did the audience, the offer or the tracking change on this date?" Never write "because" or "probably" about a cause you cannot see.
- **Do not blind-scale.** Before a SCALE call, check frequency headroom, audience size, how long the campaign has run, and whether another campaign targets the same people. If you cannot see them, type the recommendation as TEST (a small, capped step with a stop rule) and say what you could not see.

## Output

1. The answer in three lines (for example "460 of 988 have a dated signal: warm / 528 fit only: cold until a signal / 100 held back from B as the test"), then the removals by reason, the A, B, C counts, the signal rows kept and dropped by reason, the holdout size and how it was carved, the warm share and the verdict, and the upload and exclusion files (row counts).
2. The A queue (top 25 rows in the answer; the full list as a CSV), the drop dates (the first drop day with how many companies leave their list that day, and how many leave in the next 30 days), and what to say out loud (the holdout and the 48 hour matching).
3. **A files table** so the person knows what each file is for: file | rows | what it is | what to do with it. Upload to LinkedIn: upload-ab.csv, upload-c.csv, exclude-holdout.csv, exclude-crm.csv. Keep for the next runs: master.csv (give it to every Monday run), holdout.csv (give it to the next monthly check so the holdout is not carved again), master-carve.csv (an unchanged copy of master.csv from today; give it only at quarter end, never replace it). For a person, not for LinkedIn: a-queue.csv. Say which files can be deleted once uploaded (none of the keep files).
Monday run:
1. A one-line brief: the A companies to write to, the cards, the notes to deal owners, with 0 sent.
2. Placeholder check: band, shares of clicks and impressions, the names to exclude, the review group and the top 5.
3. Dose check: a verdict per ad set with views a person a month, new people a week, price, share reached.
4. Router: the match line, the classification counts, the cards (up to 5, in the card layout), the notes, the A queue, the CRM hand-off CSV and the weekly chain even at zero.
Quarter end: the served B against holdout counts with both sizes, A and C for reference, the signals on won deals, the proposal (or none under 5 won deals).

## Blind spots

You cannot see these. Do not guess at them. List the ones that matter under "What I could not check", and ask the user when the answer would change the result.

- Which signals exist: the user logs them; this routine does not find them.
- Who clicked: LinkedIn reports companies, never people; a card names roles.
- LinkedIn's matched member counts (typed by the user after 48 hours) and typical match rates.
- Reach, frequency and penetration, which are LinkedIn's estimates and take 24 to 36 hours to settle.
- Whether the author's plan (about 5 views a person a month, the alert line, the windows) holds for the user's account: the checks are what test it.
- Outcomes: no outcome proof exists yet for the alert line.
- Changes the user made during the window (budget, bid, audience, creative). Ask when one would explain a result.
- Goals: what each campaign is meant to achieve, unless the user says so. Ask when the stage is not clear.
